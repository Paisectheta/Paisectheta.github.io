Do you remember how earlier we used to see these American college kids had their classy portfolio websites or blogs? I kind of loved the idea. A small corner of the internet just for themselves. Nowadays people do LinkedIn... But where's the fun in that? This site for me is not just protfolio, it is also my cozy little mindspace - a submind, if you may.

The point of this design is not nostalgia for its own sake; it is to make the portfolio feel like a place that can hold projects, references, and personal context without turning into a generic landing page. I chose this design because this is the one place where I can feel like I am away from all the noise and the loudness of modern day internet. Now I know, I never experienced the original world wide web as some of our elders did, but it sure does look nicer.

## What encompasses this design philosophy

 - **Density over decoration:** Information, text and structure take priority over hero images, auto-playing videos or excessive whitespace designed for retention metrics. 
 - **Persistant spatial continuity:** Utilizing a stable multi-column frame enables to have consistancy across the website. It feels like moving through a physical bulletin board rather than sliding through endless feeds.
 - **Low-friction textility:** Light markup, fast loading times and high colour contrast that honours readability and writing above passive visual consumption.
 - **Personal taxonomy:** Organising thoughts and work by personal utility and interconnected relevance rather than algorithmic popularity or chronological feeds.

## Why this design works

- The top bar keeps the important sections visible everywhere.
- The left rail gives each page a quick local map.
- The right rail holds recurring context, so every page feels connected.

## Does this mean the modern internet is trash?

Not really... You see, modern internet has shifted from a network of destinations to a series of optimised human capture engines. Sort of like a market where every shop-owner is shouting over the other to visit *their* shop. Algorithmic feeds trade depth, individuality and spatial memory for maximum user retention. Corporate platforms further push *every* user into identical visual templates, and honestly, that turns personal identities into boring standardised profiles.

While this design isn't a rejection of modern tools; it sure is an opt-out from the noise. It reclaims the web browser as an intimate canvas for reflection, curation and deep work rather than a portal for endless scrolling.

## Quirks

 - **No responsive hamburger menus:** If the screen shrinks, the layout reflows gracefully without hiding navigation behind abstract icons.
 - **Visible structural seams:** Borders, background shades, and functional dividers are left exposed to show how the page is constructed.
 - **Static timestamping:** Articles and notes show when they were written or last updated, without live comments, likes, or view counters.
 - **Text-first media:** Images and embeds are treated as supporting references, never as full-screen backgrounds or visual fluff.

## What comes next

I’ll keep filling this site with project notes, writing, and other things worth remembering. If a page feels too empty, I’ll treat that as a sign to make it more useful instead of more decorative.
