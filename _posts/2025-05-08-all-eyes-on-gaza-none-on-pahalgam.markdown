It’s always funny to see the faces of the media change. To see how people react - or don’t react - to certain topics is almost comical. Media went from being a place to communicate to a place where people now spend their lives, chase their clout, and display their curated conscience.

## The changing face of media

Back when Israel struck Gaza, of my mere 34 followers on Instagram, about 90% had posted “All Eyes on Gaza” or some similar sentiment. You couldn’t scroll five taps without seeing flags, infographics, and powerful fonts against dramatic backdrops. Gaza was everywhere. Overnight, everyone became a geo-political analyst.

Support poured in - loud, visible, performative or not - it was there. The outrage was real, or at least, it felt real enough to fill a feed.

## A sea of infographics

And then came April 22nd. Pahalgam. A place far closer to home. An attack that killed innocent civilians in a cowardly manner. One that shook lives. That bled into silence.

No posts. No stories. No hashtags. Just a void.

At least, when the news first broke, not a single one of those previously “vocal” followers had a word to say. Not even a passive re-share. No “All eyes on Pahalgam.”

## The silence closer to home

But oh - a day or two later, the same timeline flared up again. Now, suddenly, everyone wanted war. Keyboard patriots with zero skin in the game calling for missile strikes as casually as ordering food. No pause. No thought spared for the military families, the soldiers who’d be sent to do the dirty work while they sit comfortably with popcorn and patriotism.

And when the government actually responded - with precision strikes based on intelligence - bam, another shift.

- “Children were killed!”
- “This was a failure!”
- “India messed up!”

Just like that, from war cries to guilt posts. Rage turned to regret faster than reels can load.

## From war cries to regret

Funny, isn't it? The same people who screamed for fire couldn’t handle the burn.

What this cycle proves is simple: public opinion is not driven by empathy. It’s driven by convenience. First comes silence. Then outrage. Then war cries. Then crying. A full loop of reaction with zero reflection.

## The full cycle of reaction

And here’s the thing: it’s not about choosing one over the other. It’s about being consistent. If your humanity is genuine, it shouldn’t disappear when the location isn't globally glamorous. It shouldn’t need a hashtag starter pack. And it sure as hell shouldn’t demand war if you can’t stomach the cost.

So the next time another place bleeds, ask yourself:

> Is your heart reacting - or just your algorithm?
