The second half of September starts, and it's time for Techtember. I think this is the best time to write my first takes post. Now, this one's gonna go outside the usual format a bit - I don't really want to write a full blog post right now, but at the same time, it's like a compendium of takes in one go.

## The library in a pocket

A quick search tells me Amazon released the first Kindle on November 19th, 2007. It had a genuine first-generation e-ink screen, a small keyboard at the bottom, and a whopping 250MB of storage. Absolutely wild for its time.

I remember seeing ads for the Kindle around 2015. I was in sixth grade, telling my dad how cool it would be to have one. Then, one day after classes, he handed me a small box. Inside was a Kindle 8th Generation - non-backlit, micro-USB, 16GB of storage. Honestly, that single piece of machinery single-handedly introduced me to the world.

Obviously, back then I started off asking my dad to buy me books (there was also a phase early on where I genuinely thought Kindles came preloaded with every book in the world - can you imagine my disappointment?).

But then came the era of discovering archive.org and a few other shady sites (wink). I went from reading Geronimo Stilton to The Martian to Dune in the span of a few years. The Kindle stuck with me through all of it - through COVID, through college. It's kind of insane how it's stood the test of time.

And I think that's what sets it apart. It just... did its thing. It's a companion. I still use it today. I've done some things to it since (probably a separate projects post on the way for that one), but at its core, it's still just doing the one job it was built for — being an ebook reader.

The thing about the Kindle is that it's totally distraction-free. No notifications. No noise. Nothing. Just pure book reading. And the e-ink display — oh, how much I love that!!!

So why does it even matter in 2026? Are Kindles dying? I don't see people buying them much anymore. I think there's just a lot more competition now. Between the advancement of e-ink tech (stuff like the Boox Note pushing what these screens can do) and Kindle's own move into color displays, I feel like something's been lost - that clean, nostalgic feeling these devices used to have.

## Two screens, one price tag

Alright, the iPhone Duo. Apple's first ever foldable, announced at their "Surprise and Shine" event, and also the first keynote of the John Ternus era post-Tim Cook. A lot of firsts packed into one event. Here's what's living in my head rent free.

**The front camera is cooked.**
So there's this clip going around of Ternus meeting IShowSpeed at the event, Speed's got the Duo in his hands, hyping it up, and then the phone kinda gets pulled back pretty quick. And look, I'm not saying I have proof, but my brain immediately went - it's the camera, isn't it. It's under-display, and Apple said NOTHING about it on stage. Not a word. For a company that will spend four minutes on a new shade of titanium, going silent on a camera spec is suspicious as hell. Under-display cameras have always been the weak link in every foldable that's tried it, and I genuinely think Apple just quietly hoped nobody would ask.

**The form factor kills the doomscroll.**
It's built like a passport. Wide when open, short when closed. And short-form vertical content - Reels, Shorts, TikTok - just does not want to live on a screen shaped like that. I think this actually changes how people use it day to day, less mindless vertical scrolling, more "open it up like a book" behavior. Obviously there's always gonna be that one guy watching Shorts stretched out on his laptop, that person exists no matter what device you hand them, but for the average user I think the shape itself pushes behavior a certain way.

**The price is not for you. And that's fine.**
Starting around $2,000/€2,339 going up to nearly €3,839 for the top storage. Like MKBHD said, this is a phone for a very particular sect of people, it's not really a "everyone should buy this" device. And I don't think it needs to be. You don't need to buy it unless you genuinely want to spend the money for the sake of spending it. If you want in on the ecosystem, there's a whole lineup of other products that'll do the job for way less.

**I don't like the colors. That's it, that's the take.**
White and dark blue. Just... not it for me. Moving on.

## Programs, machines, and the fights that made me

**Tron**

I've been following this franchise for a while now, and honestly Ares was insane. Jared Leto playing a program sent out of the Grid into the real world, first contact between humans and actual AI beings, it just works for me on a level most sci-fi doesn't even attempt. What gets me about Tron as a whole is how almost dystopian the idea is if you actually sit with it. A digital world running parallel to ours, with its own rules, its own class systems, its own version of life and death, and it never feels like it was slapped together for a cool visual. The entire thing has been thought through properly, the light cycles, the Grid, the programs having identity and purpose, it all fits into one coherent universe instead of just being neon for the sake of neon. That's rare for a franchise this old to still be expanding on and not feel like it's coasting. Oh and, you might've noticed stuff about tron all over my website :\).

**Real Steel**

And then there's Real Steel, which is just... the feel good movie. No dystopia, no grand universe to sit and dissect, just a washed-up boxer, a kid, and a scrappy robot that shouldn't work but does. It's the kind of movie you don't put on to think, you put it on because you know exactly how it's gonna make you feel by the end, and it still gets you every time. Sometimes that's all a movie needs to be.
